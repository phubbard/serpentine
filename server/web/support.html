Serpentine — Support

Serpentine plans motorcycle rides on curvy, quiet roads — loops and out-and-backs — and hands them to Apple Maps for voice guidance. For electric bikes it also plans charging stops.

Contact

Email pfh@phfactor.net with questions, bug reports or a ride that came out wrong (a screenshot and where you started helps).

Can I use it to just get somewhere?

Yes. Pick "Go somewhere", choose your destination and turn on "Just get me there": Serpentine takes the fastest route, freeways included, and still plans any charge stop you need. Leave that off and it finds the better roads instead, within whatever extra time you allow.

Can I plan by time instead of distance?

Yes — switch the plan screen from Distance to Time and say how long you have. Serpentine aims to bring you back under that time, and counts any charging stop against it.

I'm low on battery. What do I do?

Tap the bolt in the top bar, tell it what charge you have left, and Serpentine lists the public chargers it thinks you can still reach — sorted by how much of your battery the ride there would take, not by how close they are. Those are different lists: a charger over a hill can cost more than one twice as far on the flat, because climbing costs charge and so does going fast.

Each one shows what it would take and what you'd arrive with. Tap it and you get the route that spends the least charge getting there, which is usually not the quickest one. Anything beyond what's left in the pack is marked rather than hidden, and if nothing at all is in range you'll still see the nearest — somewhere to be towed to, or to aim for if you can nurse a few more percent out of it.

The estimates lean cautious, but they are estimates, made from a charge figure you typed in. Leave yourself room.

Can I keep a ride I liked?

Yes. Tap Save on any planned ride and it goes to your saved list, where you can give it a star rating, write notes about it — gravel on a turn, the charger that was always busy — and record when you last rode it. Rides you haven't ridden yet live in the same list, so it doubles as a to-do list of roads worth trying.

A saved ride keeps the route exactly as it was planned, so it opens the same way every time and works without a signal. Saved rides live on your device only: they are never uploaded, and there is no account to sync them to. Deleting the app deletes them.

Where it works

The United States, including Alaska and Hawaii. Starting points outside the US can't be planned yet, and a ride that would cross a border stops at it.

Requirements

iPhone or iPad with iOS 18.4 or later, and an internet connection to plan. Navigation uses the Apple Maps app.

Why does Apple Maps show several stops?

Apple Maps plans its own route between points, so Serpentine gives it stops along the planned roads to keep it on them. Tap to continue after each one.

How does Serpentine know if a charger is working?

It doesn't, for certain — nobody does. It checks what riders have reported to Open Charge Map: whether a charger is listed as in service, when it was last confirmed working, and whether people recently failed to charge there. A stop reported out of service is replaced. Treat the rest as a hint, not a promise, and take the backup seriously.

How accurate are the charging estimates?

Deliberately cautious: Serpentine would rather suggest a stop you didn't need than strand you. Stops are at public Level 2 J1772 and Tesla destination chargers, charging at the bike's 6.6 kW. Charger information comes from the U.S. Department of Energy and Open Charge Map, and can be out of date; check a charger before you depend on it.
